# Development audit, October 1, 2026

The trained model artifacts were fitted on earlier months only. Every training label ends before the cutoff, and fitting has an additional one-day availability buffer before the forward month. Mean/scale parameters use training samples only. Forward calibration excludes labels extending beyond that forward month. Seven feature formulas were checked against the live inference implementation. Labels overlap heavily, so 30,000–83,000 pooled rows are not independent observations; those counts must not be interpreted as that many independent trades.

In forward March–June development calibration, the logistic classifier's log loss was 0.6739, 0.6859, 0.7141 and 0.6968. An earlier-training class-prior constant achieved 0.6639, 0.6991, 0.6861 and 0.6774. Lower is better. Brier scores agree: the classifier improves on the constant in April only. This is evidence against promoting the present classifier, despite some high short-window portfolio scores. Coefficient signs also change across expanding fits. No calibration threshold was tuned after these results.

The original inference cache keyed only the artifact path. Replacing that file could keep stale coefficients and availability metadata. Inference now reads the artifact contents and caches the parsed exact content, so replacements are detected. A regression check changes coefficients and then the availability boundary at the same path. All 71 tests pass. Batch 03's archived source retains the implementation used for its original results; those results have not been overwritten.

The drawdown diagnostic compares three development policies against a 99% buy-halt threshold solely to estimate the latch's effect. This threshold is unacceptable for deployment and is not a candidate configuration. Baseline loss worsens from 8.30% to 21.72%, with 23.42% maximum drawdown. The three-hour/twelve-hour trend improves from a 4.83% loss to a 1.31% loss, with 14.85% drawdown. Volatility-sized twelve-hour/two-day trend remains a 2.47% loss, with 5.63% drawdown. Removing the halt does not establish a profitable edge. No live risk setting or reset rule was changed.

Calibration evidence: `reports/research/batch-03/calibration.json`. Halt evidence: `reports/research/halt-diagnosis/{registry,results}.json`. Both read development data only. Actual test data remains absent; no new candidate validation has been run.

The expected-return ridge challenger also underperforms an earlier-training mean predictor on forward squared error in March, May and June; April is its only improvement. Its continuous forward net returns are -5.60%, -2.57% and -1.29%, with doubled-cost results of -9.34%, -4.58% and -2.22%. The same March–June allocation-matched passive benchmark gains 7.42%. None qualifies for validation.

Original classifier policies were replayed continuously without refitting or changing thresholds. Their March–June returns are -1.66%, -2.72% and -0.09%; doubled-cost returns are -4.44%, -4.53% and -0.44%. Cash remains better in net return, and the classifiers remain poor on calibration. Continuous results do not replace the archived monthly-reset evidence; both are preserved.

Batch05's first renderer missed a date-bound definition for its custom forward period. Fitting and all portfolio outputs had already been saved. The report was repaired from saved curve/trade/signal files only; no model or price replay was repeated for that repair. The original source is archived, and the repair is recorded in report provenance. The current trainer supplies the correct custom bounds for future reproduction.

Replacing generic rational-arithmetic population deviation with a finite-float, two-pass `math.fsum` implementation speeds the seven-feature synthetic microbenchmark by approximately 3.27x (3,000 evaluations). Across 100 synthetic histories the maximum feature difference was 2.78e-17. Replaying March's volatility-sized classifier after the change produced the identical strategy-order count and identical net return, drawdown, fees, turnover and ratios. This verifies that specific replay and formula equivalence, not a universal claim that every floating-point boundary is unchanged. Runtime evidence and replay fingerprints are recorded in feature-runtime.json and feature-equivalence.json. Source integrity hashing now includes every Python module in the bot package, including the new numerical helper and live engine. All 79 tests pass.

All six relative-strength rotation policies also have negative continuous January–June returns. Their full protocol and failed results remain under batch06. No new candidate has been evaluated on validation or the real test.
